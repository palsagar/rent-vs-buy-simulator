Buy vs. Rent Financial Simulator

Should you buy a home or rent and invest the difference? This simulator models two financial strategies side-by-side so you can compare outcomes with your own numbers.

How this works

▸The two strategies
A
Buy

You purchase property with a mortgage. Your asset is the home value. Outflows include the down payment, closing costs, monthly mortgage payments, property levy, insurance, and maintenance.

B
Rent + Invest

Rent and invest what buying would have cost: the down payment and closing costs up front, plus the monthly difference whenever renting is cheaper.

▸Net Value

A home appreciating in value looks great — but you're also paying mortgage interest, property levy, insurance, and maintenance. Net Value captures the full picture:

Net Value = what you'd walk away with − everything you paid in

For Buy, what you'd walk away with is the sale price minus the remaining mortgage, selling costs and any capital-gains tax, plus anything the buyer invested along the way and any tax savings from deducting mortgage interest and levies. For Rent, it is your investment portfolio after capital-gains tax.

Everything you paid in is the same total for both: the up-front costs, plus each month the larger of the rent and the cost of owning, because the cheaper side invests the difference. So both numbers are often below zero.

This tells you which strategy actually leaves you wealthier. A scenario can have higher asset growth but lower net value if the costs are steep enough.

▸Breakeven

The breakeven point is the year when buying overtakes renting + investing in net value. Before this point, the renter is wealthier. After it, the buyer pulls ahead.

No breakeven? That means one strategy dominates for the entire simulation period. If you're planning to sell before the breakeven year, the dominant strategy may flip.

▸Monte Carlo confidence

The main charts assume growth rates play out exactly as configured. But nobody can predict 30 years of returns. The How sure is this? section runs hundreds of simulations where property appreciation, equity growth, and rent inflation vary randomly each year using bell-curve distributions centered on your inputs.

Instead of “Buy wins by $142K,” you see: “Buying wins in 68% of simulations.” The fan chart shows the range of possible outcomes, and the tornado chart reveals which parameters matter most.
